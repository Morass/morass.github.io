Apothecary Brush Drying Rack — hangs brushes bristles-down

A rack that dries makeup brushes the way every brush-care guide says to dry them: hanging HEAD DOWN over a drip tray. A brush stood bristles-up runs water into the ferrule, the glue softens and the brush sheds — this is the one makeup problem with a real failure mode behind it, and nothing prints a rack that fixes it. The grip is a WEDGE SLOT, not a hole: handles run about 6–12 mm and no single bore fits them, so each of the six slots tapers 13 → 5 mm over 22 mm and every brush catches at its own diameter, at about 9.4° per side, well inside PLA's friction angle. 172 mm of measured hang clears a 190 mm brush. The rail lifts straight off the two posts to load, and the tray is then a plain open dish you tip and rinse. 2 parts, 176 × 78 × 219 mm, 301 g, no supports, no hardware, no glue.

Cauldron Makeup Organizer — a bowl with no corners, no supports

A three-legged cauldron that sorts makeup, and the bowl is the whole idea: it is a truncated sphere, so there is no corner anywhere inside it for powder to pack into. A 2.6 mm wall runs into a 3 mm floor through a 4 mm fillet rather than a crease, and one wipe reaches every part of the inside. Brushes stand in it bristles-up and fan out against the curve; a 4-way cross drops in to quarter the bowl for jars, sponges, compacts and hair ties, and lifts out to leave a plain brush cup. The three legs are a separate trivet ring the bowl drops into on a 45 degree cone, because legs cast under the bowl print as a horizontal overhang between them. 3 parts, 92 x 90 x 77 mm assembled, 117 g, no supports, no hardware, no glue — assembly is dropping one part into another.

Dragon Wing Palette Rack — eyeshadow palettes on edge, no supports

An eyeshadow-palette rack whose fins are the finger bones of a dragon wing. Palettes stand on edge like books, so you read the spines and pull one out one-handed instead of excavating a stack. Five 16.5 mm slots take anything up to an 18 mm palette, with 100 mm of clear depth between the front rail and the back spine and a 76 mm shortest fin. Every segment of the fin profile is upward-facing, so a 112 mm tall part prints with ZERO supports. 1 part, 118 x 128 x 112 mm, no supports, no hardware, no assembly.

Drain Hair Snake — Barbed Clog Remover

Flat flexible barbed drain stick that rakes hair clogs out of a shower, bath, or sink drain. Push the tip down the drain, twist, and pull — backward-pointing harpoon barbs grab the hair and drag it back out. One-piece print, no supports, no assembly. Finger-loop handle doubles as a hang hook and a stop so it cannot vanish down the drain.

Frost Crystal Casket — makeup organizer, no supports

A block of glacier ice with a quartz cluster growing out of the back, and the cluster is the working part: all seven crystals are bored straight through, so a brush pushed into a shard stands bristles-up while its handle hangs in the box below — which is why a 120 mm and a 220 mm brush end up standing at the same height. Bores are cut to measured stock: Ø27 for mascara, two Ø23 that neck down to Ø14 so a lipstick rests on the shoulder, and four brush bores at Ø16 and Ø11. There is not one blind hole in the model and the well underneath is a single cavity with a 4 mm cove where the floor meets the wall, so nothing has a square corner to pack powder into. 3 parts, 132 × 114 × 100 mm, 298 g, no supports, no hardware, no glue.

Great Helm Makeup Organizer — knight's helm, no supports

A 13th-century flat-topped great helm that is a makeup organizer on the inside. The crown is open and a lift-out liner drops onto the rim carrying the whole bore field, so brushes stand out of the helm like a crest: six brush bores go straight THROUGH so the handle hangs in the cavity and a 120 mm and a 220 mm brush stand at the same height, three tube bores (Ø27 mascara, two Ø23 lipstick) neck down instead of bottoming out, and an 88 × 24 mm palette slot rests a compact on an open 8 mm ledge. Not one bore is blind, so powder falls through into a smooth drum you tip out and rinse. 2 parts, 134 × 125 × 150 mm, 307 g, no supports, no hardware, no glue.

Wizard Tower Makeup Organizer — 4 lift-apart tiers

A wizard's tower that sorts makeup. Brushes hang bristles-up from five wedge bores through the roof cone, so each one catches at its own handle diameter and a 120 mm and a 190 mm brush stand at the same height. The balcony deck takes a mascara, two lipsticks and three pencils; the plinth is a deep open bin for polish and jars. Every one of the eleven openings is a THROUGH hole and the bin floor is coved into the wall, so powder falls through instead of packing into a blind corner: the tower lifts apart into four pieces you tip and rinse under a tap. No supports, no glue, no hardware — every joint is a triangular tongue that drops into a V-groove.
