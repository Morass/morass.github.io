Bathroom / Makeup organizers

Apothecary Brush Drying Rack — hangs brushes bristles-down

A rack that dries makeup brushes the way every brush-care guide says to dry them: hanging HEAD DOWN over a drip tray. A brush stood bristles-up runs water into the ferrule, the glue softens and the brush sheds — this is the one makeup problem with a real failure mode behind it, and nothing prints a rack that fixes it. The grip is a WEDGE SLOT, not a hole: handles run about 6–12 mm and no single bore fits them, so each of the six slots tapers 13 → 5 mm over 22 mm and every brush catches at its own diameter, at about 9.4° per side, well inside PLA's friction angle. 172 mm of measured hang clears a 190 mm brush. The rail lifts straight off the two posts to load, and the tray is then a plain open dish you tip and rinse. 2 parts, 176 × 78 × 219 mm, 301 g, no supports, no hardware, no glue.

Bathroom / Makeup organizers

Cauldron Makeup Organizer — a bowl with no corners, no supports

A three-legged cauldron that sorts makeup, and the bowl is the whole idea: it is a truncated sphere, so there is no corner anywhere inside it for powder to pack into. A 2.6 mm wall runs into a 3 mm floor through a 4 mm fillet rather than a crease, and one wipe reaches every part of the inside. Brushes stand in it bristles-up and fan out against the curve; a 4-way cross drops in to quarter the bowl for jars, sponges, compacts and hair ties, and lifts out to leave a plain brush cup. The three legs are a separate trivet ring the bowl drops into on a 45 degree cone, because legs cast under the bowl print as a horizontal overhang between them. 3 parts, 92 x 90 x 77 mm assembled, 117 g, no supports, no hardware, no glue — assembly is dropping one part into another.

Frost Crystal Casket — makeup organizer, no supports

A block of glacier ice with a quartz cluster growing out of the back, and the cluster is the working part: all seven crystals are bored straight through, so a brush pushed into a shard stands bristles-up while its handle hangs in the box below — which is why a 120 mm and a 220 mm brush end up standing at the same height. Bores are cut to measured stock: Ø27 for mascara, two Ø23 that neck down to Ø14 so a lipstick rests on the shoulder, and four brush bores at Ø16 and Ø11. Tenshu Makeup Organizer — Japanese castle keep, 3 lift-off storeys

A Japanese castle keep (tenshu) that sorts makeup. Every roof is the lid of the storey under it: the base holds four compacts on edge in a lift-out comb plus a polish bay, the second storey has three coved lanes for a mascara, lipsticks and pencils lying down, the third a tweezer lane and two cells for lashes, pins and rings.
