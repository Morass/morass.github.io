Raise the Skyward Watch

The crane swings a course of stone across the wall and one tap sets it. Anything that overhangs the course below crumbles into the gorge and the wall is narrower from then on — but a course that loses nothing is laid true, the crane sends another stone up, and the next course may oversail the one below. A badly climbed wall can still be won back.

Forty walls across seven marches, each with its own width, load and swing table. Three of the marches carry a rule of their own: a plinth too narrow to be careless on, a buttress course that braces the wall wider — but only if you lay it true — and a battlement that is not crowned until the last course lands clean. Three Open walls have no top at all.

A crowned wall keeps the ten fastest crowns; the Open walls keep the ten tallest climbs. Every row carries its replay, so any of the ten can be watched — nothing sends a score, a climb sends the climb and it is re-run to work the height out.
